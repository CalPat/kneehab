Changes v12.01 — standing protocol added to Daily Habits
  • Clinical driver: right knee medial joint-line discomfort under prolonged standing, resolves quickly with rest. Mechanism: right glute med inhibition (v11.95 dx) → Trendelenburg lurch under unconscious sustained loading → trunk drifts laterally over right leg → varus moment at right knee → medial compartment compression. v11.95 cues fixed the cued-training pattern (knee valgus on Step Down); unconscious standing-day pattern (lateral lurch → medial compression) was uncovered.
  • NEW · Standing Posture Check (Daily Habits, ⭐ priority): every 20–30 min during long standing. Three checks, ~5s each: (1) head centered over hips (no lateral lurch); (2) weight even on both feet; (3) light right glute engagement. Set a timer or attach to existing cues. Discipline is the dose.
  • NEW · Right-Side SL Standing Micro-Dose (Daily Habits, ⭐ priority): 10–20s × 5–10 per day. Lift left foot, active right glute med holds pelvis level — no drop, no lurch. Builds carryover from cued single-leg sets to sustained single-leg standing. Highest-leverage intervention in the standing protocol. Reassessment criterion: clean 30s right SL stance with no compensation = right glute med inhibition resolving.
  • Why this matters more than cued exercises: training is ~50 min/day of perfect loading; standing-day is 6+ hours of unconscious loading. Compensation patterns live in the unconscious window — that's where the work is.
  • What did not change: Day A, Day B, Day R, Activity, Collagen Block all unchanged. v12.0 KB additions (Front Rack Squat, Suitcase Carry, Plyo Primer) and rotation/cuts stand. Right-side gating on Step Down · Split Squat · SL Wall Sit stands — this finding *validates* the gating, not relaxes it.

Changes v11.95 — right-side glute med bias on knee tracking work
  • Diagnostic finding: right-side groin-to-medial-knee tightness (cat-cow, child's pose with reach) plus right-adductor activation as stabilizer in bird-dog support, plus right-knee tracking failure on slant board step-down. Three independent signals → right glute med weak → adductors compensate → knee valgus on loading. Classic PFPS upstream pattern.
  • Slant Board Step Down: stance-leg knee tracks straight over middle toe. If a side compensates (knee valgus, trunk lean, pelvis drop), regress that side only — lower slant angle, drop to flat step, or sub Overcoming ISO Step-Up until tracking cleans up. Use mirror or phone for visual feedback.
  • Hip Abduction: top knee faces forward throughout — if it rolls toward ceiling, TFL steals the rep instead of glute med. Cue: toes slightly down at the top.
  • Single-Leg Glute Bridge: pelvis stays level — unloaded side does not drop. If it does, that's the failing-side signal.
  • Bird Dog (all variations): if support-leg adductors fire as primary stabilizer (groin to inside-knee tension), pelvis is rotating and obliques aren't bracing. Brace ribcage-to-pelvis first, lift only as far as support side stays quiet. Quality > range.
  • No new exercises, no schedule changes. Cue refinements only — applies the asymmetry rule to specific compensation patterns. Reassess at 2 weeks.

Sun Salutation A2–3 rounds · one breath per movement
v13.31 — full per-position coaching detail (Feel / Watch). v13.3 — replaced the Cat-Cow → Down Dog → Up Dog flow; same spinal-articulation + scap targets, richer (poses as loading, Mitchell). One breath per movement; the only deliberate hold is Down Dog. Move at a pace where the breath fills the transition — chasing glide and gradual range, not a max stretch. Almost nothing here loads the knee compressively.

Hold timing: everything flows one breath (~3–5s) except Down Dog = 5 breaths (~20–30s); optional 2-breath Up Dog hold if hips / t-spine are tight. Longer static holds (30s+) shift from warm-up into connective-tissue remodelling — better placed in the collagen window than pre-training (a long static hold right before lifting can transiently blunt force).

1 · Mountain (arms overhead) — inhale. Feel: even weight through the mid-foot tripod, quads lightly on (kneecaps draw up), ribs stacked over the pelvis, long reach heels→fingertips, shoulders down. Watch: ribs flaring / low-back arching as the arms rise (knit ribs down), shrugging, jamming the knees into hard hyperextension (keep them micro-soft).

2 · Forward Fold — exhale. Feel: hinge from the hips not the waist, sit bones up / back, hamstrings load, head hangs heavy. Watch: rounding the lumbar to reach the floor; locking the knees to force range (soft-bend if tight — protects the low back and the back of the knee).

3 · Halfway Lift (hands to shins, flat back) — inhale. Feel: spine long and flat, crown reaching forward, sit bones back (gentle traction), erectors + lats on, gaze just ahead of the toes. Watch: cranking the neck to look up (neck in line with the spine), letting the back round, shrugging.

4 · Plank — one breath. Feel: whole-body brace, shoulders over wrists, one line ear→heel, glutes + quads on, "press the floor, scaps wide" — the same anti-extension brace as your RKC Plank. Watch: hips sagging (lumbar dump), hips piking, shoulders drifting ahead of the wrists, head dropping, breath-holding. Regression: knees down.

5 · Lower / Chaturanga — exhale (controlled eccentric — don't pause). Feel: elbows track back alongside the ribs, lower as one rigid unit, shoulders stay at elbow height, triceps + serratus control the descent. Watch: elbows flaring wide (impingement), shoulders sinking below elbow height (cuff strain), hips sagging / piking, collapsing down fast. Regression — and the default for most: knees down (half-chaturanga).

6 · Up Dog — inhale. Feel: press the tops of the feet down so the thighs + knees lift off the floor (this is what makes it Up Dog, not Cobra), press the hands to send the chest forward and up, distribute the arch through the whole thoracic spine, shoulders down / back, collarbones broad, front of the hips opening. Watch: dumping into the low back (the #1 fault — lead with the chest, lengthen the lumbar, knit the ribs; a pinch means back off or Cobra with thighs down), shrugging, locking the elbows (micro-bend + external rotation), throwing the head back.

7 · Down Dog — exhale, HOLD ~5 breaths. Feel: inverted V, hips high and back, "press the floor away, scaps wide" (serratus + arms externally rotating), long spine is the priority over straight legs, slowly pedal the heels down to load calves / soleus + hamstrings, press through the knuckles. Watch: rounding the upper back (bend the knees generously — length beats straight legs), shoulders creeping to the ears (depress + widen), chest collapsing, hands parked too close to the feet (walk them out). Knee-friendly: bend as needed, no compressive load, and it doubles as ankle-dorsiflexion work.

8 · Halfway Lift — inhale (same as #3). 9 · Forward Fold — exhale (same as #2). 10 · Mountain, arms overhead — inhale (same as #1 — rise on the inhale, under control, to avoid the head-rush). Repeat 2–3 rounds.

Safety First: sharp / pinching pain — usually a low-back pinch in Up Dog or shoulder pain in Chaturanga — means back off or take the regression (Cobra / knees-down) that round. Dull stretch and effort are fine; sharp is a stop signal.
90/90 Lift-Offsfront + back leg · both sides · 5 reps or 3 × 5s holds
v14.0 NEW — replaces Shin-Box Get-Ups. The get-up travelled the same hip IR/ER end ranges, but passively-ish and without returning a reading; the lift-off loads them actively and tells you something. It is also the only item in the program that reads the TRANSVERSE PLANE — Sun Salute covers sagittal and shoulder, the box squat covers loaded knee flexion, the step-up covers single-leg tolerance. Nothing touched rotation.

Setup: sit in 90/90 — front leg flexed and externally rotated, shin across in front; back leg abducted and internally rotated, knee bent behind. Tall on both sit bones, hands a light kickstand rather than a lever. Front-leg lift = active hip external rotation at end range. Back-leg lift = active hip internal rotation at end range. Both are small; an inch is a real rep.

Why it is upstream of the knee: restricted hip rotation is associated with patellofemoral pain, and the mechanics are simple — rotation the hip will not supply gets taken up distally, at the tibia and the patellofemoral joint. Same chain the glute-med thread chased, different plane.

Primary: hip external rotators and posterior gluteus medius/minimus fibres (front leg); hip internal rotators, TFL and adductor group (back leg); deep hip capsule at end range. Compensations to avoid: leaning the torso back to manufacture the lift (the pelvis stays stacked), pushing off the hands, momentum, breath-holding.

KNEE-SILENCE RULE: the hip should feel the work and the knee should feel nothing at all — watch the BACK leg especially, which is knee flexion plus hip internal rotation, on the knee with the anterolateral history. Back knee talks → lower the lift or narrow the angle. Same rule the program applies to Frog Rock.

Placement: always AFTER Sun Salute A, never first — end-range active hip work on a cold hip is the one real risk in this block. First two reps easy. Regression: narrower angle, sit up on a block, or isometric hold without lifting. Progression: longer holds → greater height → light ankle weight or band.
Squat to Box5 reps · ~3s descent · TAP, do not sit · bodyweight
v14.0 NEW — a sensor, not training. No training value, and it needs none. Its only job is to make the knee report every morning against a fixed reference.

Setup: the same box that caps the KB Front Rack Squat (the v13.41 depth gate) at its current height. Bodyweight, normal stance, ~3s descent, tap the box with the glutes, stand. Tap — do not sit, do not bounce, do not rest.

Why the box and not a free squat: it makes depth a CONSTANT. A free squat varies day to day, so “felt tighter today” is unreadable — different depth AND different sensation is two moving parts and no signal. Fixed depth leaves only one thing free to vary, which is what turns an impression into a measurement.

Read all four: (1) does it reach the box as easily as yesterday — terminal flexion; (2) do you lock out fully at the top — an effusing knee loses terminal extension BEFORE it hurts, the earliest signal available; (3) catch, crunch, apprehension; (4) which way you shift, and whether that changed.

Why it earns a slot: the 2026-08-13 event was delayed-onset and was found by a test squat only AFTER the exposure had accumulated. A fixed-depth squat every morning is the instrument that surfaces creeping intolerance before it becomes a sharp episode. The ISO Step-Up cannot catch terminal-range loss.

Considered and rejected — heel-elevated full range. Heel elevation is ANKLE-friendly, not knee-friendly: the upright torso it permits shifts load anteriorly and raises both knee flexion angle and patellofemoral demand, making it the most knee-loading squat variant rather than the gentlest. DF measured 5in and was ruled out as a limiter (v13.43), so it buys range already owned. And v13.43 concluded that gating depth inside the session is meaningless while uncapped end-range compression accumulates outside it — a daily full-range squat is that exposure through the front door. It is also a worse sensor, because “full range” is a moving reference. Depth is earned on the ratchet, not taken daily.
ISO Step-Up — overcoming isometric3 × 30s each side · free foot OFF · alternate sides · fingertip on wall
v14.0 — moved here from Strength. A Baar isometric was sitting at the front of a hypertrophy session because that is where the clock put it. Tendon wants frequency and costs nothing to recover from; muscle wants recovery. This block is the frequency. Same box as the squat above — one station.

Two jobs. The day’s tendon dose (patellar and quadriceps tendon under sustained tension — Baar for synthesis; Rio et al. 2015 for analgesia, where 5 × 45s isometrics produced immediate pain reduction and reversal of cortical inhibition in patellar tendinopathy). And because it is unilateral, a left/right tolerance reading a bilateral hold cannot give.

THE FREE FOOT COMES OFF THE FLOOR — v14.0 ruling. Strain: the tendon job needs real load, and full bodyweight on one leg is what is available without equipment. Standardisation, the stronger reason: a reading needs a fixed reference — regulate weight through the free foot and a worse-feeling hold could mean the knee is worse OR that you leaned harder. Two moving parts, no signal. Balance is the real confound, not load — a light fingertip on a wall stabilises without unloading. The foot being off is also what makes the stance-hip glute-med demand real (the abductors hold the pelvis level under full bodyweight), which is exactly why this replaces banded Standing Hip Abduction rather than sitting next to it.

Intensity = knee angle and added load. Never the free foot. Angle is the primary dial — near lockout is almost nothing, 60° is a serious quad isometric, and within a step-up’s usable range deeper means more patellofemoral stress. So angle is the intensity dial AND the safety dial. Added load (KB at the chest) is the progression. Both run on the RATCHET: hold angle and load constant until a full clean cycle passes the 24-hour check, then step up. A dial you turn freely is noise, and the reading dies with it.

Primary: quadriceps and patellar/quadriceps tendon (isometric), stance-leg gluteus medius, gluteus maximus, foot intrinsics. Compensations: pelvis dropping on the free-leg side, trunk leaning over the stance leg, valgus drift, breath-holding, brushing the floor with the free foot. Right-side gating stands (v12.01). Regression: shallower angle, shorter holds, or let the free foot take weight — but then FLAG that day’s reading as modified rather than comparing it. BUILD NOTE: the hold angle has never been specified in this program, and angle determines what the exercise is. Measure what you currently hold and record it — that is the ratchet’s zero.
Dead Hang30s · passive or active · step down, do not drop
v14.0 — promoted to a daily item. It closes the upper-limb gap; everything else in the Opener is hip, knee or spine.

What it reads: shoulder capsule and cuff under traction, and the elbow extensor origin under grip load. If the elbow becomes a thing, this finds it first — before a Pull-Up does.

It is not only a sensor. 30s of hanging is genuine loading for those tissues, which matters because rest is the wrong treatment for a reactive tendon: the tissue detrains, capacity falls, and the same load provokes it again. So a grumpy elbow or shoulder gets a longer hang, not a skipped one — cut the provocative work (speed, loaded stretch, high force at end range) and keep the isometric.

Primary: shoulder capsule and rotator cuff under distraction, latissimus and thoracolumbar fascia, forearm flexors and the common extensor origin, grip. Spinal decompression as a bonus. Compensations: shrugging into a dead slump when the shoulder is irritable (add light active scapular depression), breath-holding, dropping off rather than stepping down. Regression: feet lightly on the floor taking some weight, or shorter holds. Progression: longer → active hang (scaps depressed) → one-arm-biased.

Two loaded knee practices, opposite verdicts — deep squat sitting is loaded, STATIC, END RANGE, long duration and provoked the 2026-08-13 event; rucking is loaded, CYCLIC, MID RANGE, long duration and feeds the tissue. Same joint, no contradiction: cartilage is viscoelastic, so cyclic loading pumps fluid in and out while sustained compression expresses it out and keeps it out.

REGRESSION LADDER — replaces “skip if sore”. The old rule was binary and over-conservative in one direction; skipping surrenders the good part along with the bad. Muscle soreness (diffuse, sore to press, better after warming up — quads after a ski day): ruck as normal, because low-load cyclic movement is treatment for DOMS, not a risk to it. Joint signal (stiffness, effusion, joint-line, or anything the morning Squat-to-Box flagged): DROP THE PACK, KEEP THE WALK — removes the compressive load provoking it while preserving the fluid exchange the joint wants. Sharp or anterolateral: stop, route to the 24-hour rule. Ladder: full load → lighter → no pack → shorter → stop.

Standing Posture Check ⭐Every 20–30 min during long standing
v12.01 — clinical priority. Right-side glute med inhibition produces a Trendelenburg lurch under sustained unconscious loading (trunk drifts laterally over the loaded leg), creating medial compartment compression at the right knee. Cued sets don't expose this; standing all day does. Three things to check, ~5 seconds each: (1) Head centered over hips — no lateral lurch.
