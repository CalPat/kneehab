Mobility — 5 min
Mobility Block 5 min · daily ▼
90/90 Hip Rotations10 × each side
Floor. Rotate hips to bring front shin parallel to floor, hold 3 sec. Poor hip rotation is a primary upstream driver of patellofemoral load.
Cat-Cow → Down Dog → Up Dog5 Cat-Cows · 3–5 cycles · ~90s
Hands and knees. 5 Cat-Cows (arch spine up, drop belly) — then flow directly into Down Dog (hips back, arms long, heels toward floor) → Up Dog (hips down, chest forward, arms straight). 2s hold at each end. Single setup, continuous sequence. Cat-Cow primes spinal segments before Down Dog loads the posterior chain and opens the anterior hip. v12.02 cue · Down Dog phase: "press the floor away, scaps wide" — actively protract the scapulae and push the floor away for 3–5 breaths at the top of each Down Dog. Recruits serratus anterior in the same fiber pattern as a scap push-up (~45° inverted instead of horizontal). Replaces the v12.01 Scap Push-Up prep set — same protraction work, folded into the existing mobility flow.
Kneeling Ankle Drive10 each side · 2s hold
Half-kneeling lunge position on the floor. Drive front knee forward over toes, loading through hands and upper body weight. 2s hold at end range, return. Same joint action as wall ankle dorsiflexion (tibial glide over talus, soleus and posterior capsule stretch) with better loading control — you can modulate pressure through your hands. Flows naturally from Cat-Cow and Lizard floor positions. Restricted dorsiflexion forces medial knee translation under load — a primary upstream PFPS driver.
Half-Kneeling Rotation1 × 8 each side · 2s end-range pause
Half-kneeling position (90/90 — one knee down, other foot forward). Arms extended straight forward at chest height, palms pressed together (or holding a stick for visual feedback). Rotate torso slowly to one side as far as possible without moving the pelvis. 2s pause at end range. Return to center under control. Trains hip-spine dissociation — spine rotates while pelvis stays square. Fills the active loaded rotation gap (anti-rotation work alone doesn't train rotational force production). Direct sport carryover for skiing and MTB. Compensations to avoid: pelvis rotating with torso, lumbar arching to fake range, head/eyes leading the motion, holding breath at end range. Regression: Quadruped Thread-the-Needle (less postural demand). Progression: hold a stick or light DB extended forward (longer lever) → tall kneeling (both knees down) → standing split-stance with band resistance.
Lizard Pose30s each side
Low lunge, both hands inside the front foot. Sink hips forward and slightly lateral. Back knee down. Deep hip flexor and adductor stretch under bodyweight. Replaces World's Greatest Stretch in v11.0 — deeper sustained hold targets the same tissues with greater connective tissue adaptation under the Baar model. Progression: drop to forearms when hip flexor flexibility allows.
Couch Stretch1 × 60s each side
Shin against wall behind, other foot forward. Upright torso, squeeze rear glute. Targets rectus femoris — the hip flexor that crosses the knee joint directly. Rec fem is dense and resistant — 60s drives adaptation past the elastic phase. Day B only — active recovery for rec fem after Day A loading.
Pigeon Pose60s each side
Front leg bent across the body, back leg extended. Sink hips down toward the floor. Deep hip external rotator stretch — targets piriformis and obturator group under bodyweight. 60s hold drives viscoelastic creep in dense capsular tissue (Mitchell) — past the elastic phase into adaptation. Replaces Supine Hip ER with Block in v11.0 — same target muscles, more functional loaded position. Regression: Supine Hip ER with Block (on back, knees bent, block between knees, let one knee drop outward to comfortable end-range external rotation, hold 30–45s).
Core Day A ~6 min · Day B ~7 min ▼
Bird Dog — Slow Return90s total
Extend opposite arm and leg, then return over 3–5s with control. Keep hips level — do not rotate. The slow return doubles the eccentric anti-rotation demand compared to standard bird dog. Pick this variation when you want sustained time under tension. Compensation watch — applies to all bird dog variations: if you feel the support-leg adductors firing as the primary stabilizer (groin to inside-knee tightness), the pelvis is rotating and obliques are not engaged. Brace ribcage-to-pelvis first, lift the working leg only as far as the support side stays quiet. Quality of stability > range of motion.
Bird Dog — Elbow-to-Knee90s total
Extend opposite arm and leg, then round spine to touch elbow to knee under the body, extend again. Dynamic anti-rotation through spinal flexion and extension range. Teaches spinal control through full range of motion. Pick this variation for dynamic days.
Bird Dog Row90s total
Hold leg extended. Light DB in opposite hand, row from floor to hip. Anti-rotation under dynamic upper body load — the highest-demand bird dog variation. Pick this when the other two feel easy. Progression: increase DB weight.
WLYT60s total
Prone. W (elbows bent), L (elbows 90°), Y (overhead 45°), T (straight out). Hold each position as long as comfortable. Lower trap, mid trap, scapular stabilizers. Day B only — pairs with upper body pulling.
Core Day A ~6 min · Day B ~7 min ▼
Hollow Body Hold60s total
On back, arms overhead, legs straight. Press lower back to floor, slight leg and shoulder lift. Day A only — foundational anterior core bracing. (Removed from Day B in v11.93 — replaced by Ab Wheel Rollout in the rotation.)
Side Plank90s total · both sides
Forearm and side of foot. Keep hips high. Both days.
RKC Plank60s total
Forearm plank. Simultaneously squeeze glutes, quads, fists, and brace abs. Drive elbows toward toes without moving them. Full-body co-contraction — significantly harder than a standard plank. Day B only — alternates with Ab Wheel Rollout each session.
Ab Wheel Rollout2 × 6–10 reps · 3s eccentric
Knees on pad, ab wheel under shoulders. Roll forward extending arms and torso, controlled descent (3s), pull back to start. Eccentric anti-extension under full ROM — the dynamic counterpart to isometric anterior core work. Highest anti-extension demand of any bodyweight exercise. Day B only — alternates with RKC Plank each session. Compensations to avoid: lumbar hyperextension at end range (only roll out as far as you can return under control); shoulder shrug (keep scaps depressed); knees lifting off ground. Regression: barbell + plates as implement (more friction limits ROM); short rollout to ~60% extension. Progression: from knees → tall kneeling → from toes (full standing rollout). Watch: grip on the wheel handle may aggravate lateral elbow — sub barbell if so.
Hanging Knee Raise2 × 8–10 controlled
Dead hang. Raise knees to 90° or higher, lower controlled. Primary hip flexor strength exercise — working against full leg weight under gravity. Weak hip flexors drive anterior pelvic tilt under fatigue (ski descents, long hikes), directly increasing PFPS load. Also trains grip and scapular stability. Requires bar. Progression: straight leg raise → weighted ankle.
Tuck L-Sit2 × 15–20s
Parallettes or floor. Hands beside hips, press down, lift hips with knees tucked. Isometric hip flexor + shoulder depression. Mid-level option between foot-supported and hanging. Progression: extend legs incrementally toward full L-sit.
Foot-Supported L-Sit2 × 20–30s
Seated on floor, heels on ground, hands beside hips, press down through straight arms. Posterior pelvic tilt, slight hip lift if possible. Lowest demand hip flexor option. Progression: reduce heel support height over weeks.

Copenhagen Plank — Short Lever3 × 20–30s each side
Side plank with bottom knee (not ankle) resting on bench or step. Top leg straight. Hold hips elevated through medial chain. Targets adductor magnus and longus under high demand. Progressed from Adductor Side-Lying ISO in v10.27. Apply asymmetry rule — weaker side first. Progression: advance to long lever (ankle on bench) when 3 × 30s is manageable.
Single-Leg Glute Bridge2 × 10 each · 2s hold top
On back, one foot flat, other leg extended or bent at 90°. Bridge up, hold 2s at top, lower controlled. Pelvis stays level — do not let the unloaded side drop. If the unloaded hip drops on a side, the loaded glute med is failing — that side is the priority. Apply asymmetry rule. Posterior chain integration under single-leg load. Day A only.
Hip Abduction3 × 12 weak / 2 × 12 strong · 2s hold top
Side-lying, bottom knee bent for stability. Top leg straight, raise to ~30–45°, 2s hold at top, lower controlled. Keep pelvis stacked — do not rotate backward on the raise. Top knee faces forward throughout — if it rolls upward toward the ceiling, TFL is stealing the rep instead of glute med. Cue: slight internal rotation feel at the top (toes pointing slightly down). Glute medius isolation in the frontal plane. Prevents contralateral pelvic drop and femoral adduction/IR under single-leg load — the primary upstream driver of dynamic knee valgus. Band at ankle optional for added resistance. Apply asymmetry rule — weaker side first, extra set weaker side.

Jump Rope3 × 30s · 60s rest · now lives in the Collagen Block
v12.0 NEW. Lowest-amplitude plyo entry — high strain rate per ground contact at the lowest joint cost. Osteogenic for tibia/femur (bone responds to strain rate, not just magnitude). Bonus: directly loads soleus and Achilles. Keep contacts crisp — quality > duration; 2 min of crisp jumping beats 10 min of sloppy. Phasing — WITHDRAWN (v13.14, cleaned up here in v14.6). This card used to carry a box-and-drop-jump phasing (box jump-up at weeks 4–7, drop jump 12" at 8–11, drop jump 18–24" at 12+). v13.14 removed all box work as the wrong risk profile for a PFPS history, and v14.5 accidentally left this as the only jump ladder in the app. The live progression is the gated Reactive Ladder under Strength: pogo hops → stuck-landing low broad jumps → single-leg hops. No boxes, no drops. Stop-rule: sharp knee pain or post-session effusion → drop one phase, hold 2 weeks. Progression: 3×30s → 3×60s → SL variations → double-unders.
KB Front Rack Squat3 × 8 · single KB alternating sides · 2s pause at bottom · RPE 7
v12.0 NEW — fills the bilateral knee-dominant axial-load gap. Single KB racked at one shoulder (elbow tucked, bell resting on chest/shoulder, opposite arm free). Squat to depth (full ROM if range allows, parallel minimum), 2s pause at bottom (matches Split Squat tempo), stand. Alternate sides set-by-set — asymmetric load creates anti-rotation challenge, trains core integration through the asymmetric demand. Cues: torso upright (front rack position forces this — that's the point vs goblet hold), knees track over toes, full bracing throughout, eyes forward. Progression: 3×8 clean RPE 7 for 2 sessions → +4 kg, OR transition to double KB front rack (matched pair, both bells racked at shoulders) for full bilateral axial load. Why front rack vs goblet hold: rack position keeps load closer to spine = more axial; demands more upright posture = more bracing. Goblet is a regression. Why this is bilateral and not gated on right-side reassessment: bilateral squat distributes load symmetrically — does not hide compensation the way Step Down or Split Squat would. Safe to load now.
Single-Leg Wall Sit5 × 20–30s · 45s rest
Unilateral isometric cartilage load — Bellemans stimulus under single-leg demand. Progressed from bilateral wall sit in v10.27. Start 45° flexion, build toward 60°. Dull ache acceptable; sharp or >4/10 = reduce angle. Levers: 20s→30s / 45°→60° / add light load. Right-side gated (v11.95): bodyweight only until tracking clean.
Slant Board Step Down2×10 primary · 2×8 secondary · v12.0: rotate with Split Squat
10cm step Phase 1. Lower opposite foot over 5s, 2s pause at bottom (foot hovering, not touching), return. Hand on wall → unassisted. The 2s pause converts eccentric to eccentric-plus-isometric — directly mimics sustained ski loaded position. Weaker side pre-activation: before each set on the weaker side, hold single-leg stance for 5s — level pelvis, glute med actively holding — then begin the descent. Track quality, not volume: stance-leg knee tracks straight forward over middle toe. Compensations to watch — knee diving medially (valgus, glute med failing), trunk leaning over standing leg (Trendelenburg compensation), pelvis dropping on opposite side. If any of these show up on a side, regress that side: lower the slant angle, drop to a flat step, or sub the Opener’s ISO Step-Down Hold on that side only until tracking cleans up. Mirror or phone propped up gives faster feedback than verbal cueing. Right-side gated (v11.95): bodyweight only until tracking clean. Progression levers (2×2 rule): 10cm→15cm→20cm→25cm / 3s→4s eccentric / bodyweight→KB at chest. Isometric modifier (soreness >2/10): lower to working angle, hold 30–45s each side instead of full eccentric reps.
Split Squat Progression2 × 8–10 each side · 2s pause at bottom · v12.0: rotate with Step Down
Default: slant board under front foot — shifts load anterior for quad/patellar tendon emphasis, compensates for dorsiflexion limitation, keeps torso upright. Rear leg extended throughout — not bent to 90°. Extended rear leg stretches rectus femoris under load. 2s pause at bottom increases tendon adaptation and motor control at the sticking point — standard prescription from v11.0, not a soreness modifier. Levers (2×2 rule): partial→full depth / standard→3s eccentric / slant board→flat→rear foot elevated / bodyweight→KB goblet→KB each hand. Progression: flat ground when dorsiflexion allows → forefoot on step for deeper ROM and greater hip flexor/glute demand. Right-side gated (v11.95): bodyweight only until tracking clean. Isometric modifier (soreness >2/10): hold at bottom position 30–45s each side instead of reps.
Curtsy Squat3 × 8 weak / 2 × 8 strong · 2s pause at bottom
Rear foot crosses behind and outside stance foot. Stance-leg glute med resists femoral adduction — the primary training stimulus. 2s pause at bottom for tendon adaptation. Keep stance-leg knee over 2nd–3rd toe. Apply asymmetry rule. Optional swap for Lateral Step-Up. Regression: hold door frame, reduce depth. Progression: 3–4s eccentric → light goblet weight. Isometric modifier (soreness >2/10): hold at working depth 20–30s each side.
Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift2 × 8 each side · BW or light KB
Stand on one leg, hinge at the hip — free leg extends behind as counterbalance. Lower torso until roughly parallel with the floor, then return to standing. Primary: glute max, hamstrings (lengthened position), erector spinae. Watch for: lumbar rounding, hip hiking, trunk rotation. Regression: Kickstand RDL (rear foot toe-touch). Progression: KB in contralateral hand → heavier KB → eventual bilateral KB RDL. v12.0: Hamstring Slide removed from program — SL RDL covers unilateral hamstring eccentric, and KB-loaded SL RDL is the progression that closes the gap.
Single-Leg Calf Raise — Soleus2 × 12–15 each · 3s down
Knee bent ~30°. Isolates soleus — primary plantarflexor under all knee-flexed positions: wall sit, step down, split squat, ski descent, cycling. Promoted to main Knee block in v11.0. Levers: flat→step edge / 3s→5s eccentric / bodyweight→weighted.
KB Suitcase Carry2 × 40m per side · heaviest KB · Day B finisher
v12.0 NEW. Loaded unilateral carry — spinal axial compression under sustained load + anti-lateral-flexion oblique work + directly trains right-side QL / glute med pattern under load. Knee-spared, no CNS cost — the safest possible loaded addition. Setup: heaviest KB you own in one hand, walk 40m at normal pace, switch hands, return. That's one set. Rest 90s, repeat for 2 sets total. Cues: walk tall, ribcage stacked over pelvis, opposite shoulder pulled down (anti-lateral-flexion — the loaded side wants to tip you sideways; resist it). Do not lean to counterbalance — let the loaded side's obliques and QL do the work. Breathe normally throughout — don't hold breath. Why this is safe for the right-side asymmetry: intentional asymmetry trained equally each side is *fixing* the QL / glute med pattern, not hiding compensation inside a "bilateral" lift where the strong side dominates. Progression: both sides 40m unbroken with good posture → +4 kg.

Opener — Tendon & Joint every morning · 7 of 7 ▼
Bird Dog — anti-rotation sensor~30s · brace FIRST · note the quiet height · sensor, not training
v14.7 — restored, and restored as a SENSOR. v14.0 cut Bird Dog on the premise that once-weekly was “sufficient for an owned anti-rotation pattern.” That premise has now been tested and failed. It comes back at ~30s, and it comes back in the same category as Squat to Box: this has no training value and it does not need any. Its whole job is to report a number every morning.

What it reads. Hands and knees, brace ribcage-to-pelvis first — exhale, ribs down, 360° — then extend the opposite arm and leg. The number is the height at which the support-side groin stays quiet. Groin-to-inside-knee tightness appearing is the read: it means the anti-rotation demand has exceeded what the brace supplies, and the support-leg adductors have been recruited to hold the pelvis. Note the height, stop, switch sides.

Why the sensation is a threshold and not a tightness. A length restriction cannot switch on at end range of the opposite leg — the support-side hip angle never changes during the lift, so nothing on that side lengthens. What rises is the rotational moment the trunk must resist. Tightness you can turn on and off by changing the load on a different limb is a muscle being recruited, not a tissue being short.

Do NOT try to train inside the quiet range. That was the v14.7 draft error, and it recreates exactly what v14.0 dissolved the old Warm-Up for: a deliberately sub-maximal item that progresses nothing. Bodyweight anti-rotation cannot be loaded — no external mass, short lever — so it fails on the two dials that scale. The threshold is moved in Strength, by the Renegade Row. It is measured here.

Primary (as a reading): internal and external obliques and transverse abdominis as the intended stabilisers; support-leg adductors as the substitute you are watching for. Compensations: pelvis rotating or hiking, lumbar extension, breath-holding, rushing. Progression: there is no dose progression — the number rising IS the progression. Quiet range creeping higher over weeks means the threshold has moved, which is the anti-rotation work in Strength reporting back.
Sun Salutation A2–3 rounds · one breath per movement
v13.31 — full per-position coaching detail (Feel / Watch). v13.3 — replaced the Cat-Cow → Down Dog → Up Dog flow; same spinal-articulation + scap targets, richer (poses as loading, Mitchell). One breath per movement; the only deliberate hold is Down Dog. Move at a pace where the breath fills the transition — chasing glide and gradual range, not a max stretch. Almost nothing here loads the knee compressively.

1 · Mountain (arms overhead) — inhale. Feel: even weight through the mid-foot tripod, quads lightly on (kneecaps draw up), ribs stacked over the pelvis, long reach heels→fingertips, shoulders down. Watch: ribs flaring / low-back arching as the arms rise (knit ribs down), shrugging, jamming the knees into hard hyperextension (keep them micro-soft).

2 · Forward Fold — exhale. Feel: hinge from the hips not the waist, sit bones up / back, hamstrings load, head hangs heavy. Watch: rounding the lumbar to reach the floor; locking the knees to force range (soft-bend if tight — protects the low back and the back of the knee).

3 · Halfway Lift (hands to shins, flat back) — inhale. Feel: spine long and flat, crown reaching forward, sit bones back (gentle traction), erectors + lats on, gaze just ahead of the toes. Watch: cranking the neck to look up (neck in line with the spine), letting the back round, shrugging.

4 · Plank — one breath. Feel: whole-body brace, shoulders over wrists, one line ear→heel, glutes + quads on, "press the floor, scaps wide" — the same anti-extension brace as your RKC Plank. Watch: hips sagging (lumbar dump), hips piking, shoulders drifting ahead of the wrists, head dropping, breath-holding. Regression: knees down.

5 · Lower / Chaturanga — exhale (controlled eccentric — don't pause). Feel: elbows track back alongside the ribs, lower as one rigid unit, shoulders stay at elbow height, triceps + serratus control the descent. Watch: elbows flaring wide (impingement), shoulders sinking below elbow height (cuff strain), hips sagging / piking, collapsing down fast. Regression — and the default for most: knees down (half-chaturanga).

Watch: dumping into the low back (the #1 fault — lead with the chest, lengthen the lumbar, knit the ribs; a pinch means back off or Cobra with thighs down), shrugging, locking the elbows (micro-bend + external rotation), throwing the head back.

Watch: rounding the upper back (bend the knees generously — length beats straight legs), shoulders creeping to the ears (depress + widen), chest collapsing, hands parked too close to the feet (walk them out). Knee-friendly: bend as needed, no compressive load, and it doubles as ankle-dorsiflexion work.

Safety First: sharp / pinching pain — usually a low-back pinch in Up Dog or shoulder pain in Chaturanga — means back off or take the regression (Cobra / knees-down) that round. Dull stretch and effort are fine; sharp is a stop signal.

ISO Step-Down Hold — yielding isometric3 × 30s each side · alternate sides (the switch is the rest) · free foot off the floor · fingertip on wall for balance
v14.5 — RENAMED. It was never a step-up. The v12.0 exercise was a genuine step-up: free foot on the floor, press UP into an immovable surface at ~80% effort — an overcoming isometric, force directed upward. v14.0 took the free foot off the floor, which left you standing on the box under full bodyweight with nothing to press into. That position is the bottom of a step-down, held still: a yielding isometric. The name survived a mechanics change that invalidated it, and a name that claims the opposite of what the exercise does hides the exercise.

Overcoming vs yielding is not semantics. In an overcoming hold, effort is the intensity dial — you choose 80%. In a yielding hold, effort is not available at all: bodyweight and knee angle set the tension. That is exactly why the v14.0 ruling is right that intensity is angle and load, never the free foot. It is also why 3 × 30s is a bigger ask than the same numbers were in v12.0 — yielding holds fatigue faster than overcoming holds at matched relative load.

CITATION CORRECTION (v14.5). Rio et al. 2015, cited here since v14.0 for the analgesic effect, used overcoming isometric knee extension at 70% MVIC in a leg-extension machine, 5 × 45s. This is a yielding hold at an angle set by geometry. The analgesic effect plausibly transfers — yielding holds have their own tendinopathy evidence — but the study no longer describes the protocol being run, and the program should not pretend otherwise. Baar’s synthesis rationale (high tendon strain sustained ~30s, repeated) is unaffected and still applies.

THE BOX IS A CEILING ON THE RATCHET (v14.5). The free leg hangs, so the stance knee can only bend until the free foot lands. On the current 6in step that is roughly 40° of knee flexion, maximum. Past it the free foot touches down, takes weight, and the reading is contaminated — the exact two-moving-parts failure the free-foot-off rule exists to prevent. So the angle dial has about 40° of travel and then stops: the next progression is a taller box or a KB at the chest, never a hovering foot faking depth. 40° also sits below the steep part of the weightbearing patellofemoral stress curve, which climbs hard past ~60° — so a left/right difference showing up at this angle is a capacity or control signal rather than joint compression.

Deliberate overlap with Step Down, stated plainly (v14.5). This and the Strength block’s Step Down (slant board) are the same movement pattern in two contraction modes — yielding isometric here, daily, for tendon frequency and analgesia; eccentric there, three times a cycle, for capacity. That is a coherent tendon-loading split. It should be visible, not hidden behind a name that claimed the two were opposites.

Two jobs. The day’s tendon dose — patellar and quadriceps tendon under sustained high tension, the Baar target. And, because it is unilateral, it reads left/right tolerance asymmetry, which a bilateral hold cannot.

THE FREE FOOT COMES OFF THE FLOOR — v14.0 ruling, unchanged. Strain: the tendon job needs meaningful load, and full bodyweight on one leg is the strain available without equipment. Standardisation, the stronger reason: a reading needs a fixed reference. Regulate weight through the free foot and a worse-feeling hold could mean the knee is worse or that you leaned harder — two moving parts, no signal. Balance is the real confound, not load; fix it with a light fingertip on a wall, which stabilises without unloading. The free foot being off is also what makes the stance-hip glute-med demand real — the abductors have to hold the pelvis level under full bodyweight — which is why this replaced banded Standing Hip Abduction rather than sitting alongside it.

The ratchet. Hold angle and load constant until a full clean cycle passes the 24-hour check, then step up: angle first, to the ~40° box ceiling, then a KB at the chest. One dial at a time. A dial you turn freely is not a dial, it is noise, and the reading dies with it.

Primary: quadriceps and the patellar/quadriceps tendon (isometric), stance-leg gluteus medius (weightbearing pelvic control), gluteus maximus, foot intrinsics. Compensations to avoid: pelvis dropping on the free-leg side (the fault you are resisting), trunk leaning over the stance leg, knee drifting into valgus, holding the breath, letting the free foot brush the floor to cheat. Right-side gating stands (v12.01): bodyweight on the right until it holds as cleanly as the left. Regression: shallower knee angle, shorter holds, or — explicitly — let the free foot take weight, but then FLAG that day’s reading as modified rather than comparing it. Progression: longer holds at the same angle → deeper angle → KB at the chest.

Safety: sharp or retropatellar pain ends the hold — this is a tendon dose, not a pain-tolerance test. An ache that is still there at the next morning’s 24-hour check means the angle was too deep, not that you need to push harder.

BRACE-FIRST READING VARIANT (v14.7). Take one hold per side having braced ribcage-to-pelvis first — exhale, ribs down, 360° — and compare the left/right gap against your usual reading. Gap shrinks with the brace → the asymmetry is STRATEGY, not capacity, and strategy re-patterns far faster than tissue rebuilds. Gap unchanged → capacity or tolerance, and the ratchet plus an extra set on the weaker side is the answer. Either way, log it as a braced reading rather than folding it into the normal series — it is a different test. This also feeds entry gate #2 on the Reactive Ladder directly.

Renegade Row2 × 6–8 each side · set ends at the first rep the pelvis rotates
Plank on two KBs, feet wide, row one bell to the hip without letting the pelvis rotate. This is the loaded Bird Dog: same demand, an actual external load on an actual lever, and zero knee cost.

Read this as a threshold, not a tightness. A length restriction cannot switch on at end range of the opposite leg — the support-side hip angle never changes during the lift, so nothing on that side lengthens. What changes is the rotational moment the trunk has to resist. Tightness that appears and disappears with load on a different limb is a muscle being recruited. You raise that threshold by working near it under load; you do not raise it by staying below it.

Why the adductors, and why this is the fix. The anterior oblique sling runs external oblique → anterior abdominal fascia → contralateral internal oblique → and terminates in the adductors of the opposite side. The adductors are in the same sling as the obliques. So when the oblique end runs out of capacity, load transfers to the adductor end of the same chain — that is not a random substitution, it is the chain doing what chains do. Training the obliques under rotational load is therefore treating the actual thing, and stretching the adductors is treating the far end of it.

Primary: internal and external obliques and transverse abdominis under high anti-rotation demand, quadratus lumborum, lats and mid-traps on the rowing side, glutes and quads holding the plank. Compensations to avoid: hips rotating with the row — that is the entire exercise, and its failure point; hips piking up; the plank sagging into lumbar extension; rushing the row. If a hip lifts, the bell is too heavy or the feet are too narrow. Regression: wider feet → knees down → unloaded plank shoulder-taps. Progression: narrow the feet first (shrinking the base is the biggest single jump in demand) → heavier bell → pause at the top. Terminator: the first rep where the pelvis rotates ends the set — a quality terminator, same class as Step Down and Split Squat.

Split Squat 2s2 × 6–10 each side · 2s pause · depth-capped · stop at first compensation
v14.82 — the target position, named. v14.83 — the rear leg corrected to ATG. This card long said only “stationary split stance, lower with control, drive up” — a tempo and a stop-rule for a movement whose load location is set entirely by geometry it never specified. In a split squat the geometry is the exercise: torso angle, front shin angle, rear-leg length and lateral base decide which tissue receives the load. Effort decides none of it.

THE LONG REAR LEG — why, and why it is the target rather than a style choice (v14.83). Ben Patrick / ATG puts the rear leg far back, and that is correct here for a mechanical reason, not an aesthetic one. Hip extended plus knee flexed puts rectus femoris under two-joint stretch — it is the only quadriceps head crossing the hip. A short, stiff rectus femoris pulls the patella superiorly into the trochlea and raises patellofemoral compression. That is an upstream PFPS driver, and it is precisely what the ATG foundation means by “anterior chain loading, deep range of motion — addresses upstream PFPS drivers.”

It is also the only loaded hip-flexor LENGTHENING in the program. The Couch Stretch in R is unloaded and runs once per cycle. The S “Hip Flexor” item (Tuck L-Sit / Hanging Knee Raise) is hip flexion shortening under load — the opposite demand. Nothing else lengthens this tissue under load, three times a cycle. That is what this exercise is for, and it is why it is not simply a third quad exposure.

Why the posterior tilt is the mechanism, not a cue — and why it matters MORE at a long stance. Stance length only becomes rear-hip stimulus if the pelvis stays tucked. Let it untuck and the identical stance length goes into lumbar extension instead: the pelvis tips anteriorly and the spine finishes the movement the hip could not. A long rear leg without the tuck is not an ATG split squat, it is a back extension wearing a hip-flexor label. The tuck is what decides which tissue receives the rear-leg load. The Opener’s 90/90 Lift-Offs and the RKC Plank build this control; here is where it gets spent.

WHAT “TUCK” MEANS, concretely. Posterior pelvic tilt. Think of the pelvis as a bowl: let it pour forward and the tailbone lifts and the low back arches (anterior tilt); tuck it and the pubic bone rises toward the sternum, the tailbone drops under you, and the low back flattens. It is a small movement — roughly 10–15°, removing the arch rather than reversing it into a rounded back — and it is set standing tall before the descent, never hunted for at the bottom.

The cue that works in this position: squeeze the glute of the REAR leg. Gluteus maximus extends the hip and posteriorly tilts the pelvis in the same contraction, so one cue buys both — and the rear hip then extends by muscle rather than by hanging on the joint. Ribs down at the same time, bottom of the ribcage drawn toward the pelvis; without that the lumbar simply extends higher up and the identical fault reappears one level north.

Find the range: stand with a hand on your low back, arch and feel the gap open under your fingers, then tuck and feel it flatten against your hand. Hold the flat end. Confirm it under load: the stretch should sit at the front of the rear hip and thigh. If you feel it in the low back instead, the tuck is gone — identical position, different tissue.

Why it is the whole variable: the pelvis is the shared attachment. Iliacus and rectus femoris run pelvis-to-femur, so if the pelvis is free to rotate forward, the femur can travel back without those tissues ever lengthening — the pelvis just follows the leg, and a long stance buys you nothing. The tuck anchors the pelvis so that rear-leg extension has to be absorbed by tissue length instead of by pelvic rotation. That is the mechanism in one sentence, and it is why stance length without the tuck is wasted range.

Rear-leg length and front-knee depth are SEPARATE AXES. The block under the back knee caps how far the front knee flexes and how far the pelvis descends. It says nothing about how far back the rear leg reaches. Do not trade one for the other: the depth gate stays exactly as it is (per side, right higher, one notch per clean cycle), and the stance lengthens on its own ratchet.

MOVEMENT. Set the stack standing — ribs down, tail tucked. Descend straight down: the pelvis traces a vertical line, the rear knee drops toward the block behind you. 2s pause. Drive up through the front-leg mid-foot and heel; the front leg does the work. Stand tall without snapping into hip hyperextension.

WHERE IT SHOULD BE FELT. Front-leg quad — distal third, around and above the kneecap — plus front-leg glute, diffuse and burning by the last rep. And a strong, clear stretch across the front of the REAR hip and down the front of the rear thigh — that second one is rectus femoris, and it is half the exercise, not a side effect. Nothing anywhere as a point. Diffuse muscle and diffuse stretch are right; punctate is wrong, every time.

WHAT SHOULD END THE SET. The front-leg quad and glute running out. If the set ends on balance, on the low back, on a knee point, or on grip, the exercise measured something other than what it is being scored on. This sits underneath the first-compensated-rep terminator: the terminator says when to stop, this says what should have been the limiter.

THREE CHECKABLE READS. Rear-foot unweight test — at the top, can you lift the rear foot? If not, the rear leg is driving and the front leg is not getting its dose. Vertical pelvis path — film from the side; the hip should trace a line, and any forward arc means the torso is pitching. Knee over middle toe — film from the front, same fault set as Step Down.

Primary: quadriceps of the front leg (vasti and rectus femoris, highest demand at the paused bottom) and the patellar tendon; gluteus maximus of the front leg through the drive; gluteus medius and the frontal-plane stabilisers holding the narrow base; iliopsoas and rectus femoris of the REAR leg, loaded at length — the unique contribution.

Compensations — each with its own fix, not a generic regression. Point under or around the front kneecap = patellofemoral load past current tolerance → raise the block a notch on that side. Point at the patellar tendon just below the kneecap = tendon, not joint, so this is a dose question rather than a position one; sharp ends the set, dull and load-proportional is the adaptation. Pinch or hard compression in the low back = the rear hip ran out of extension and the pelvis tipped. This is a hip-flexor length fault, not a back fault, and shortening the stance is a temporary regression rather than the answer — re-set the tuck first, and shorten only as far as the tuck will hold. Permanently capping the stance to avoid the tilt prevents the adaptation that removes the tilt. Groin or adductor on the front leg = base too narrow, front knee drifting medially, adductors taking frontal-plane duty → widen the stance laterally. Rule this out before reading it as a clinical signal on the right — a narrow base manufactures the same report. Hamstring and glute only, no quad = torso pitched forward and shin vertical; it has turned into an RDL, which item 3 already does better. Toes gripping or calf cramping = balance failing into an ankle strategy → hand on the wall. Rear-thigh cramp = rectus femoris cramping at end range; back off the stance one step for that set, it is a capacity report and it recedes.

REGRESSIONS, in the order to try them. 1. Widen the stance laterally — free, and it fixes the most. 2. Hand on a wall or rail — removes the balance tax so the quad can actually be the limiter. Step Down has always carried this regression and this exercise did not. 3. Shorten the stance toward rear-knee-under-hip and re-set the tuck — this is the regression, not the target. 4. Raise the rear-knee block, per side.

PROGRESSION — and the ORDER is the safety rule (v14.83). ATG progresses range; this program’s old ladder progressed load. Long stance plus heavy bells plus depth is exactly where the lumbar risk is real, so the dials are ordered: (1) stance length at bodyweight, until the tuck holds at full length for a clean cycle → (2) hand off the wall → (3) load: goblet or front rack → (4) double bell. Front-knee depth runs on its own separate ratchet (the block, per side) throughout. Never lengthen the stance and add load in the same cycle.

ELEVATE THE FRONT FOOT, OR USE A SLANT BOARD? Two different tools on two different axes (v14.84). A slant board is a wedge: it raises the heel relative to the forefoot, cuts the ankle dorsiflexion required, lets the knee travel further forward, and so adds anterior knee load at the same depth — it changes where the load goes. Flat front-foot elevation — a plate or step, foot level — is the ATG deficit: it moves the floor down and buys more depth, more front-knee flexion and more rectus femoris stretch through the knee end — it changes how far you go.

The deficit is not a separate tool. It is the depth ratchet continued past zero. Proof: elevate the front foot by any height, raise the rear block by that same height, and nothing changes — the whole movement is simply translated upward. The block under the rear knee and the deficit are the same axis pointing opposite directions. So this is one dial, not two: lower the block one notch per clean cycle, and when the block reaches the floor and more depth is wanted, the front foot comes up. Per side, same ratchet, same gate. Reaching for a deficit before that is a second, uncalibrated way to move the one variable this knee is gated on.

The slant board is DECLINED here — not parked. Three reasons, and none of them is that it is unsafe. (1) Its stated rationale on the old card was compensation for a dorsiflexion limitation, a premise v13.43 killed by measurement (5in knee-to-wall; DF ruled out as a limiter, which is why Kneeling Ankle Dorsiflexion was cut in v14.0 and is not coming back). (2) Its live effect — more anterior knee load — is already delivered by two calibrated ratchets that exist here, the depth block and the load ladder, so the wedge is a third and uncalibrated route to the same variable. (3) Step Down is already on a slant board, one slot up this list: the tool is deployed, on the exercise built for it. A second one under the exercise whose unique job is the rear leg doubles a correctly-placed tool and dilutes what this slot is for. If more rectus femoris stretch is wanted, the free dial is stance length — already step 1 of the progression order, and it costs nothing. Load position is a direction lever too: front rack keeps the torso vertical and holds the movement knee-dominant; two bells hanging lets you go heaviest but pulls the torso forward and drifts it hip-dominant.

What this exercise is for, stated plainly. Three items in this session load the quad and patellar tendon — KB Front Rack Squat (axial, box-capped), Step Down (unilateral eccentric) and this — and this is the weakest of the three at that job, because base and balance demands cap the load below what the other two carry. It earns its slot on the two things nothing else in S does: the rear hip and rectus femoris loaded at length, and the loaded lunge pattern that transfers to ski, hike and scramble. Read it as an anterior-chain lengthening movement that also loads the front quad, not as a third squat.

v14.6 NEW — a one-off screen, not a recurring item. Run it once, warm, and let the result decide whether anything belongs in the program at all. It exists because “pelvic mobility” bundles three different things that need opposite treatments, and because a restricted hip and an uncontrolled hip look identical in movement.

Why it is upstream of the knee. Restricted hip rotation is associated with patellofemoral pain, and the mechanism is simple — rotation the hip will not supply gets taken up distally, at the tibia and the patellofemoral joint: femoral adduction plus tibial internal rotation under single-leg load, which is dynamic valgus. The four tissues this program trains have four different dose–response curves, and only muscle needs rest days. Muscle: tension and volume, 48–72h recovery → Strength, 3×/cycle. Tendon: strain × duration, saturates in ~10 min then ~6h refractory, near-zero recovery cost → wants frequency, so the Opener and the Collagen Block. Bone: strain rate and novelty, ~8h to resensitise → short bouts split across the day (rope + ruck). Cartilage: cyclic compression, avascular → walking, riding, rucking. The old clock-based sort is what put three quad isometrics in three different blocks.
v14
The Opener runs every morning — before Strength, and on every other day too
Five items, equipment-light, 7 of 7 days: Sun Salutation A → 90/90 Lift-Offs → Squat to Box → ISO Step-Down Hold (3 × 30s/side, free foot OFF) → Dead Hang. On Strength days add RKC Plank and go straight into the session. It is not prep — it is the day's tendon and joint loading, and the reading comes free. No Opener requirement before Cardio: the first ten minutes of a ride, ruck or ski IS the warm-up. Baar sequencing (isometric before impact) is now preserved across the day rather than inside the session.

01
Mobility is non-negotiable — daily on both days
Full mobility block runs on both days. Couch Stretch on Day B only (active recovery for rectus femoris after Day A loading).
02
Collagen Block is the single amplified window — afternoon, every day it has a load to attach to
Take collagen + vit C + creatine during the eating window (early afternoon for 16:8 IF), then load 45–60 min later. On an activity day that genuinely loads tendon — a real MTB ride, ruck, hike, ski — dose pre-activity and the activity is that session’s anchor (v14.3). No loading event, no dose (v14.8): collagen without mechanical load is metabolised as general protein, so there is no before-bed rest-day version of this.
03
All collagen: 2+ hours after any protein meal
Leucine blunts connective tissue synthesis response. Allow 2.5 hrs after a full meal for leucine clearance (2 hrs minimum after a light meal). 16:8 IF example: lunch 12 PM → collagen 2:30 PM → load ~3:15 PM. Collagen triggers a mild insulin response — keep it inside the eating window.
04
Morning sequence is recommended on both days
Mobility → Core → strength progression warms tissues in the right order. No longer collagen-dependent but the sequence is still optimal.
05
Easy movement is the default Collagen Block loading option
After collagen, just move — a walk or an easy bike within the synthesis window. Cyclic compressive loading feeds avascular cartilage via fluid exchange, and casual walking or cycling delivers it. No fixed seat height or hard stop — the rigid Bellemans Bike protocol was dropped in v13.07 (too onerous, never followed). Rotate isometric / yoga / loaded-walk options for tissue variety.
06
Zone 2 cycling unrestricted
Zone 1 unrestricted on all days. Zone 2 rides can serve as the Collagen Block loading option if timed within the collagen window.

Tracked at the top of Daily Habits. Counts easy bike/spin, PM rucks, Activity Day, dedicated ride/row/hike/swim, loaded carry walks. Excludes ambient walking. No zone gating — intuitive intensity.
