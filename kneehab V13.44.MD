## The correction

**1. Glute med tests symmetric on every available measure.**

| Test | Result | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated hip abduction | Symmetric | 2026-05-06 |
| Side-lying abduction endurance | Symmetric | 2026-08-13 |
| 30s single-leg stance | Symmetric, no compensation | 2026-08-13 |

The v12.01 resolution markers are met. The valgus chain requires an under-recruiting glute med; three tests argue against one.

**2. A "30 minutes a day in a deep squat" practice was running outside the program.**

That's the driver. The test squat that produced the pain landed on top of **accumulated** end-range patellofemoral compression — not a novel one-off. It explains what the compensation story never did well:

- **Delayed onset** — consistent with accumulated compression, not a single rep
- **~60 min resolution** — a fluid-shift response, precisely the reasoning the v12.01 note applied to the standing medial pain
- **Why it appeared now** — the exposure is new, the knee isn't

**Verdict: a tolerance finding, not a compensation finding.** Better prognosis, and it responds to exactly what the box progression already does.

## Why a long static deep hold is the wrong tool here

### v13.44 correction: depth is not the villain

Patellofemoral joint **force** does rise steeply with flexion — but **stress** does not track it.

| Activity | PFJ force |
|---|---|
| Level walking | ~0.5× bodyweight |
| Stairs | ~3–4× BW |
| Bodyweight squat at 90° | ~3–5× BW |
| Deep squat | up to ~7–8× BW |

**Contact area grows with flexion** — the patella seats into the trochlear groove, roughly 2 cm² near extension to 4–6 cm² by 90° and beyond. Past ~90–100° the **quadriceps tendon contacts the femoral trochlea** and shares load (the "wrapping effect"). **Peak patellofemoral stress therefore sits nearer 90° than at maximum depth.** The joint is well designed for the deep position; v13.43 overstated this.

**So the case against the practice is not peak load. It is three other things:**

1. **Sustained beats peak.** Cartilage is viscoelastic. Brief passes load it and let it rebound; a minutes-long hold drives progressive fluid exudation and degrades load distribution the longer you stay. Static and cyclic are different stimuli, not different amounts of one — the Bellemans point, intact.
2. **The tissue is untrained.** Very deep flexion engages surfaces — including the odd facet on the extreme medial patella, which only articulates past ~135° — that a knee which has never gone there has never conditioned.
3. **Accumulated dose.** 30 min/day is a lot of anything for a joint with a symptom history.

Novel surface × long duration is the risk, not the peak number.

**And the program had already dropped it.**

Deep Squat Hold was cut in v13.03. It returned outside the program at roughly six times the dose.

**What it's actually buying:** the practice targets ankle dorsiflexion and hip flexion range. Ankle DF measured **5 in knee-to-wall** — above threshold, already ruled out as a limiter anywhere — and hip range is covered across `W` and `R`. Marginal benefit low, cost now demonstrated.

This is a **dose problem, not a ban.**

## Changes

### 1. Deep Squat Sitting — new capped action habit in `D` (5 actions now)

`START ~5 min/day TOTAL in 30–60s bouts · block-supported · not one long hold`

- **Cyclic, not static.** Bouts with movement between them, not one accumulation.
- **Earn duration like the squat box** — hold the dose for a full clean cycle (no next-morning elevation on the 24-hour check), then increase.
- **Regression:** sit on a low block or yoga block. Preserves the hip and ankle position at a materially lower knee flexion angle, which is where the patellofemoral cost lives. Heels elevated also reduces demand.
- **Progression:** lower the block first, longer bouts second, total duration last.
- **Stop rule:** any sharp anterolateral sensation, or discomfort appearing after standing up, means the dose was too long.

> **The point:** gating depth inside the Strength session is meaningless while uncapped end-range compression accumulates outside it.

### 1b. The box is not a "safe angle" (v13.44)

A cap near parallel sits close to where patellofemoral **stress** actually peaks — contact area and the quad-tendon wrap buffer the *deeper* positions, not the mid-range ones. **Capped is not unloaded.**

Do not model this as "deep = dangerous, parallel = safe." **The 24-hour response check is the arbiter, not the degree measurement** — which is exactly why the gate was built as a response test rather than an angle.

### 2. Ceiling test reinterpreted

Same test, different meaning. It now reads **tissue tolerance**, not glute-med recruitment — which makes it *more* informative, since tolerance is what graded exposure actually moves.

- Box drops a notch per clean cycle → tolerance is building.
- Ceiling won't move for **2–3 consecutive cycles** → escalate to in-person assessment.

### 3. Warm-Up glute-med items revert to maintenance

v13.42 promoted Suitcase Carry, Standing Hip Abduction, Copenhagen and Bird Dog to "primary upstream knee intervention." **Overstated.** They stay — right-biased, daily, worth doing well — but they are not the lever on the knee.

### 4. Glute-med volume question: closed

**12 dedicated + ~15 indirect exposures per cycle**, against three symmetric tests. More is definitively the wrong lever. This does not need revisiting.

**24-Hour Response Rule** — morning after each `S`: first ten steps + one slow sit-to-stand, 0–10. Baseline within 24h = acceptable. Still elevated = raise the box, no progression this cycle.

**SL Glute Bridge + psoas lock.** **Frog Rock → `R` Hips Tight.**

**Still rejected:** Nordic curls · anterior-pelvic-tilt correction · standalone daily hip-mobility routine · Cossack squat.

**Escalate out of programming** on: swelling, night or rest pain, giving way, locking or catching, or inability to fully straighten.
