# kneehab v13.15 — clarify Suitcase Carry load guidance (why it feels easier than the side plank)

**Date:** 2026-06-24
**Type:** Minor (notes/guidance only — no exercise added, removed, or re-prescribed)

## Driver

User: "Side plank feels a lot harder than the suitcase carry — am I under-loading the carry, or is it just different?" Answer is **both**, and the notes now explain it so the warm-up carry doesn't get judged against the wrong yardstick.

## The physics (now baked into the app)

Both train the lateral chain (QL, obliques, lateral hip) by resisting side-bending, but through completely different levers:

- **Side plank** = long-lever, **near-maximal** isometric. Whole bodyweight on a long moment arm ≈ **~200 N·m** of lateral-core torque — close to max, which is why it burns and fails in 30–60 s.
- **Suitcase carry (16 kg)** = short-lever task. Bell hangs ~20 cm from the spine, so ≈ **~30 N·m**, only **~10–15% of max** — an activation/endurance dose.

**The gap is the lever, not a loading mistake** — even a maximal one-arm carry can't reach side-plank torque (~100 kg one-handed would be needed). So the warm-up carry is *deliberately* sub-maximal.

## Why the carry was still the right swap (v13.1) despite feeling easier

1. **More specific to the v12.01 problem** — it's *loaded standing*, so it rehearses the unconscious-standing pattern (stance-leg glute med holding the pelvis level). A horizontal side plank never trains that.
2. **It out-scales the side plank** — side plank is capped at bodyweight on a fixed lever; the carry has no ceiling (grab a heavier bell), so it's the better *progressive* lateral-core strengthener.
3. **Shoulder-sparing** — a chunk of the side plank's difficulty is the down-side shoulder supporting bodyweight, which isn't the training target.

## Activation vs. strength split (the takeaway)

| Slot | Dose | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm-Up carry** | ~16 kg floor, bump to 20–24 kg+ if easy | Activate QL/obliques + stance-leg glute med; v12.01 standing-pattern rehearsal |
| **Strength finisher carry** | Heaviest KB, progressive | Genuine high-intensity lateral-core strength (out-scales side plank) |
