# kneehab v14.84 — Split Squat: the rear leg corrected to ATG

**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Scope:** one exercise (`sS_split`), both live surfaces. Supersedes v14.82, same session. No item count change (`S` stays 18), no structural change.

---

## The correction

v14.82 spec'd the bottom position as **"rear knee under the hip, or barely behind it."** That is wrong, and Ben Patrick / ATG — a named Foundation in `CLAUDE.md` — puts the rear leg far back for a reason v14.82 failed to make.

### Why the long rear leg is the TARGET, not a style choice

**Hip extended + knee flexed puts rectus femoris under two-joint stretch.** It is the only quadriceps head that crosses the hip. A short, stiff rectus femoris pulls the patella **superiorly into the trochlea** and raises patellofemoral compression.

That is an **upstream PFPS driver** — precisely what the ATG foundation entry means by *"anterior chain loading, deep range of motion... addresses upstream PFPS drivers."* The long rear leg has a direct mechanism on the target pathology. It is not a mobility nicety.

### It is also the only loaded hip-flexor LENGTHENING in the program

Verified against `index.html`:

| Item | Block | What it actually does |
|---|---|---|
| Couch Stretch | `R` | Rectus femoris lengthening — **unloaded, once per cycle** |
| "Hip Flexor" (Tuck L-Sit / Hanging Knee Raise) | `S` | Hip flexion **shortening** under load — the opposite demand |
| **Split Squat, long rear leg** | `S` | **Loaded lengthening, 3×/cycle** — nothing else does this |

That is what the exercise is for, and it is why it is not merely a third quad exposure.

### Where v14.82 went wrong on the fault, too

v14.82's fix for the low-back pinch was **"shorten the stance."** That treats the symptom. The pelvis tips because the hip flexors are short — and this exercise is the thing that *builds that length*. Permanently capping the stance to avoid the tilt **prevents the adaptation that removes the tilt.**

Corrected: shortening the stance is a **temporary regression**, held only until the tuck holds at length.

## What SURVIVES from v14.82 unchanged

- **The posterior tilt is the mechanism, not a cue** — and it matters **more** at a long stance, not less. A long rear leg without the tuck is not an ATG split squat, it is a back extension wearing a hip-flexor label.
- Front shin travels forward, knee ahead of the ankle over the middle toe.
- Torso vertical (pitched forward it becomes the Kickstand RDL, item 3).
- Feet on train tracks at hip width, never inline.
- The set should **END on the front-leg quad and glute** — under the v13.4 terminator.
- Diffuse muscle right, punctate wrong. Three checkable reads. Per-fault regressions.

## The conflation v14.82 made

**Rear-leg length and front-knee depth are SEPARATE AXES.** The block under the back knee caps how far the *front knee* flexes and how far the pelvis descends. It says **nothing** about how far back the rear leg reaches.

v14.82 capped the rear leg for a reason (lumbar) that only justified capping the front (knee). The depth gate stays exactly as it is — per side, right higher, one notch per clean cycle — and the stance now lengthens on its own separate ratchet.

---

## Target position (v14.83)

- **Front shin forward** — knee ahead of the ankle, over the middle toe. This IS the quad/patellar load.
- **Rear leg LONG, rear knee well behind the hip** — dropping toward the floor *behind* you, not under you.
- **Torso vertical**, stacked over the pelvis.
- **Ribs down, tail tucked — set BEFORE the descent**, held throughout.
- **Feet on train tracks**, ~hip width laterally.
- Front foot flat, mid-foot/heel, toes relaxed. Rear foot ball-down, heel high — kickstand, not driver.
- Front-knee depth capped by the block, per side (v13.41/42).

## Where it should be felt

Front-leg quad (distal third) + front glute, diffuse. **Plus a strong stretch across the front of the REAR hip and down the front of the rear thigh** — that is rectus femoris, and it is **half the exercise, not a side effect.** Nothing anywhere as a point.

---

## THE ORDER IS THE SAFETY RULE (new)

ATG progresses **range**. The old ladder here progressed **load**. Long stance + heavy bells + depth is exactly where lumbar risk is real. So the dials are ordered:

1. **Stance length at bodyweight** — until the tuck holds at full length for a clean cycle
2. Hand off the wall
3. **Load** — goblet or front rack
4. Double bell

Front-knee depth runs on its **own separate ratchet** (the block, per side) throughout.

> **Never lengthen the stance and add load in the same cycle.**

### Regressions, in try-order

1. Widen the stance laterally
2. Hand on a wall or rail
3. **Shorten the stance toward rear-knee-under-hip AND re-set the tuck** — *this is the regression, not the target*
4. Raise the rear-knee block, per side

### Front-foot elevation: parked, not deleted

In ATG the front foot goes on a plate as a **deficit** — to reach more front-knee flexion. Front-knee depth is the gated variable on this knee, so the deficit waits behind the block ratchet. It is a depth tool, and depth is not a free dial.

Separately: the old orphaned card justified a slant board as *"compensation for a dorsiflexion limitation"* — a premise **v13.43 killed by measurement** (5in knee-to-wall; DF ruled out as a limiter, which is why Kneeling Ankle DF was cut in v14.0).

### New compensation added

**Rear-thigh cramp** = rectus femoris cramping at end range. Back off the stance one step for that set — a capacity report, and it recedes.

## What this exercise is for, stated plainly

Three items in `S` load quad + patellar tendon — KB Front Rack Squat (axial, box-capped), Step Down (unilateral eccentric), and this — and this is the **weakest of the three at that job**, because base and balance demands cap the load below what the other two carry.

It earns its slot on the two things nothing else in `S` does:
- **The rear hip and rectus femoris loaded at length**
- The loaded lunge pattern that transfers to ski, hike and scramble

**Read it as an anterior-chain lengthening movement that also loads the front quad — not as a third squat.**

## Appendix — what "tuck" means (added before commit)

**Posterior pelvic tilt.** The pelvis is a bowl. Let it pour forward → tailbone lifts, low back arches (anterior tilt). **Tuck it → pubic bone rises toward the sternum, tailbone drops under you, low back flattens.**

Small movement — ~10–15°. Removing the arch, not reversing it into a rounded back. **Set standing tall BEFORE the descent**, never hunted for at the bottom.

### The cue that works here: squeeze the REAR-leg glute

Gluteus maximus extends the hip **and** posteriorly tilts the pelvis in the same contraction — one cue buys both, and the rear hip then extends by muscle rather than hanging on the joint.

**Ribs down at the same time.** Without it the lumbar just extends one level higher and the identical fault reappears at the bottom of the ribcage.

### Find the range

Stand, hand on your low back. Arch — feel the gap open under your fingers. Tuck — feel it flatten against your hand. Hold the flat end.

### Confirm it under load

The stretch should sit at the **front of the REAR hip and thigh**. Feel it in the low back instead and the tuck is gone — *identical position, different tissue.*

### Why it is the whole variable

The pelvis is the shared attachment. Iliacus and rectus femoris run **pelvis-to-femur**. If the pelvis is free to rotate forward, the femur can travel back **without those tissues ever lengthening** — the pelvis just follows the leg, and a long stance buys you nothing.

The tuck **anchors the pelvis** so rear-leg extension has to be absorbed by tissue length instead of pelvic rotation.

> **Stance length without the tuck is wasted range.**

# v14.84 — front-foot elevation vs slant board: two different axes

v14.83 blurred these into one "front-foot elevation is parked" note. They are different tools doing different jobs.

| Tool | What it is | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Slant board** | A **wedge** — heel raised relative to forefoot | Cuts required ankle DF → knee travels further forward → **more anterior knee load AT THE SAME DEPTH**. Changes *where* load goes |
| **Flat front-foot elevation** | The ATG **deficit** — foot level, whole foot on a plate | Moves the floor down → **more DEPTH**, more front-knee flexion, more rectus femoris stretch via the knee end. Changes *how far you go* |

## The deficit is not a separate tool — it is the depth ratchet continued past zero

**Proof:** elevate the front foot by height H, raise the rear block by the same H → **nothing changes**; the whole movement is translated upward. The block and the deficit are **the same axis pointing opposite directions.**

So it is one dial, not two:

> **Lower the block one notch per clean cycle. When the block reaches the floor and more depth is wanted, the front foot comes up.**

Per side, same ratchet, same gate. Reaching for a deficit before that is a second, uncalibrated way to move the one variable this knee is gated on.

## The slant board is DECLINED here — not parked

Three reasons, none of them "it is unsafe":

1. **Its rationale is dead.** The old orphaned card justified it as compensation for a dorsiflexion limitation — a premise **v13.43 killed by measurement** (5in knee-to-wall; DF ruled out as a limiter, which is why Kneeling Ankle DF was cut in v14.0).
2. **Its effect is already calibrated twice.** More anterior knee load is delivered by the depth block and the load ladder. The wedge is a third, *uncalibrated* route to the same variable.
3. **Step Down is already on a slant board** — one slot up the list. The tool is deployed, on the exercise built for it. A second one under the exercise whose unique job is the **rear** leg doubles a correctly-placed tool and dilutes this slot.

**If more rectus femoris stretch is wanted, the free dial is STANCE LENGTH** — already step 1 of the v14.83 progression order, and it costs nothing.
