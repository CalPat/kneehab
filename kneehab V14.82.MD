# kneehab v14.82 — Split Squat: the target position, named

**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Scope:** one exercise (`sS_split`), rewritten on both live surfaces. No structural change, no item count change (`S` stays at 18).

---

## The finding

Two live surfaces carried the Split Squat, and **neither said where the load goes**:

- `index.html` → `src-strength` hub card
- `index.html` → `BLOCKS.S` step `sS_split`

Both read: *"Stationary split stance. Lower with control, 2s pause, drive up."* — a tempo and a stop-rule for a movement whose load location is set entirely by geometry the card never specified.

Everything about *position* — torso angle, shin angle, stance length, rear-leg length — lived only in the **orphaned** `src-knee` card ("Split Squat Progression"), which no hub tab points at. **The good content was in the dead card.**

Split squat load distribution is not a property of the exercise. It is a property of four setup choices:

| Lever | → knee/quad | → hip/glute |
|---|---|---|
| Torso | vertical | pitched forward |
| Front shin | travels forward | stays vertical |
| Stance length | shorter | longer |
| Front foot elevation | raised heel | flat |

Effort decides none of it.

---

## The ruling: knee-dominant front leg, rear hip as a capped secondary

### Target position — the bottom

- **Both knees ~90°** as the reference; the block sets the real floor, per side.
- **Front shin travels forward** — knee ahead of the ankle, over the middle toe. *This is the quad/patellar-tendon load. Wanted, not a fault.*
- **Rear knee under the hip, or barely behind it.** Well behind = stance too long = the low back pays. Ahead of the hip = too short = the rear hip gets nothing.
- **Torso vertical**, stacked over the pelvis. Pitched forward it becomes the Kickstand RDL, which is already item 3.
- **Ribs down, tail slightly tucked — set BEFORE the descent**, held throughout.
- **Feet on train tracks**, ~hip width apart laterally. Never inline.
- Front foot flat, weight mid-foot/heel, toes relaxed. Rear foot ball-down, heel high — a kickstand, not a driver.

### Why the posterior tilt is the whole design

Stance length only becomes **rear-hip stimulus** if the pelvis stays tucked. Let it untuck and the identical stance length goes into **lumbar extension** instead — the pelvis tips anteriorly and the spine finishes the movement the hip could not.

The tuck is not a cue. It is **the mechanism that decides which tissue receives the rear-leg load.** The program already builds this exact control in the Opener's 90/90 Lift-Offs and the RKC Plank; this is where it gets spent.

### Movement

Set the stack standing → descend **straight down** (pelvis traces a vertical line, rear knee drops toward the block) → 2s pause → drive up through the **front-leg mid-foot and heel** → stand tall without snapping into hip hyperextension.

---

## Where it should be felt

**Front-leg quad (distal third, around and above the kneecap) + front-leg glute — diffuse, burning by the last rep. A clear stretch across the front of the REAR hip and thigh.**

**Nothing anywhere as a point.** Diffuse muscle is right; punctate is wrong, every time.

### New: what should END the set

The front-leg quad and glute running out. If the set ends on **balance, low back, a knee point, or grip**, the exercise measured something other than what it is being scored on.

This sits *underneath* the v13.4 first-compensated-rep terminator: **the terminator says when to stop; this says what should have been the limiter.**

### Three checkable reads

1. **Rear-foot unweight test** — at the top, can you lift the rear foot? If not, the rear leg is driving.
2. **Vertical pelvis path** — film from the side; the hip traces a line. Any forward arc = the torso is pitching.
3. **Knee over middle toe** — film from the front. Same fault set as Step Down.

---

## Compensations — each with its own fix

| Report | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Point under/around the front kneecap | PFJ past current tolerance | Raise the block a notch that side; then lengthen the stance slightly |
| Point at the patellar tendon | Tendon, not joint — a *dose* question, not a position one | Sharp ends the set; dull and load-proportional is the adaptation |
| Pinch in the low back | Rear hip ran out of extension, pelvis tipped — **a hip-flexor fault, not a back fault** | Shorten the stance, re-set the tuck |
| Groin/adductor, front leg | Base too narrow, knee drifting medially | **Widen the stance laterally.** Rule this out before reading it as a right-side clinical signal — a narrow base manufactures the same report |
| Hamstring/glute only, no quad | Torso pitched forward, shin vertical — it became an RDL | Restore the vertical torso |
| Toes gripping, calf cramping | Balance failing into an ankle strategy | Hand on the wall |

---

## Regressions — in the order to try them

1. **Widen the stance laterally** — free, and it fixes the most.
2. **Hand on a wall or rail** — removes the balance tax so the quad can actually be the limiter. *Step Down has always carried this regression; Split Squat did not.*
3. **Raise the rear-knee block**, per side.
4. **Shorten the stance** if the low back reports.

## Progression — one dial at a time

bodyweight → hand off the wall → front foot on a slant/plate → goblet or front-rack KB → double bell → rear foot elevated

### THE SLANT BOARD IS NO LONGER THE DEFAULT

The orphaned card made it the default *"to compensate for dorsiflexion limitation"* — **a premise v13.43 killed by measurement** (5in knee-to-wall; DF ruled out as a limiter, which is why Kneeling Ankle Dorsiflexion was cut in v14.0 and is not coming back).

Stripped of the dead reason, the board is just **extra anterior PFJ load on a knee that is already depth-gated** and already taking quad exposure from the KB Front Rack Squat and the Step Down. Flat ground + vertical torso delivers all the shin angle needed when DF is not limiting. **The board becomes a lever, not a starting position.**

*Same pattern as the v14.72 peroneal salvage: reject the dead premise, keep only what stands without it.*

### Rear-foot-elevated is GATED, not next

It raises front-leg load and rear hip-flexor demand simultaneously. It does not open until the low-back fault is absent.

### Load position is a direction lever, not just an amount lever

Front rack keeps the torso vertical → holds it knee-dominant. Two bells hanging lets you go heaviest but pulls the torso forward → drifts hip-dominant.

---

## The cost of the ruling, stated plainly

Three items in `S` now load quad + patellar tendon: **KB Front Rack Squat** (axial, box-capped), **Step Down** (unilateral eccentric), and this. That is the third exposure of one tissue on a knee that is depth-gated for cause — and the Split Squat is the **weakest of the three at that job**, because base and balance demands cap the load below what the other two carry.

It earns its slot on what **nothing else in `S` trains**: the **rear hip in extension under load**, plus the loaded lunge pattern that transfers to ski, hike and scramble.

So the rear leg is long enough to be a real stimulus **and no longer** — capped precisely where the lumbar would start paying for it.
