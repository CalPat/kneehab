# kneehab v13.42 — Right-Knee Depth Gate + 24-Hour Response Rule

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Type:** Minor (.01) ×2 — v13.41 built the gate, v13.42 made it right-side-aware once the knee was identified.
**Supersedes:** v13.4 (RPE → RIR + observable set terminators), v13.41

---

## The event

A deep bodyweight squat on 2026-08-13 (run as a Cossack-squat limiter test) provoked **sharp anterolateral pain in the RIGHT knee at the inferior-patellar level** — in line with the bottom of the kneecap, just lateral.

| Feature | Observation | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | At depth, and **delayed** after standing | Reactive, not a mechanical block |
| Character | Sharp at depth → discomfort after | Irritative response |
| Resolution | **Fully gone in ~60 min** | **Low irritability** |
| Locking / catching / giving way | None | No structural signal |

**Conclusion:** end-range patellofemoral compression limit. Mild, positional, self-resolving — not an injury, but a real depth ceiling.

---

## It's the right knee — so this is the v12.01 thread, not a new problem

The chain from the documented right-side pattern:

> right glute med under-holding → femoral adduction + internal rotation in the stance leg → **dynamic valgus** → lateral patellar tracking → **lateral facet overload**

That chain terminates exactly where the pain was reported. One upstream driver, two downstream expressions depending on what the leg is asked to do:

| Task | Expression | Version |
|---|---|---|
| Unconscious standing (Trendelenburg lurch) | **Medial** joint-line pain | v12.01 |
| Deep loaded flexion | **Lateral** patellofemoral pain | v13.41 |

**The consequence that matters:** capping depth manages the *symptom*. It does not treat the driver. The four right-biased Warm-Up items — **Suitcase Carry, Standing Hip Abduction, Copenhagen, Bird Dog** — are the actual upstream intervention, and are now load-bearing training rather than prep.

---

## Why the delay drove the design

The discomfort arrived *after* standing, not during. That means the **v13.4 in-session terminators cannot see it**. 2 RIR, first grinding rep, first compensated rep — all assume the signal lands during the set.

> "Nothing hurt during the session" is no longer evidence the session was fine.

---

## Changes

### 1. Depth is a gated, progressed variable — `S`

Double-progression logic applied to **range** instead of load.

- **KB Front Rack Squat** — box or bench at a height confidently pain-free (start at or just above parallel). Touch-and-go, don't sit and rest. Bilateral lift, so the box stays bilateral — but **watch the right knee for valgus drift on the ascent**, the same fault the Step Down terminator catches, showing up under axial load.
- **Split Squat** — block under the back knee, **set per side**. Right gets the higher block; sides progress independently. Forcing depth symmetry on the right reinstates the pattern being extinguished.
- **Progression:** hold the height for a **full clean cycle** (all three `S` sessions, no sharp pain, no next-morning elevation) → drop one notch.
- **Mǎbù (`W`)** — pinned at or above the current box height. Sustained isometric, so it sits *above* the cap, never at it.

**Do not probe for the edge.** Repeatedly loading to the limit of a reactive patellofemoral joint is how a one-off becomes a six-week problem.

### 2. 24-Hour Response Rule — governing gate above the in-session terminators

Each morning after an `S` day, before moving properly: first **ten steps** out of bed, one slow **sit-to-stand**, note anterior knee **0–10**.

| Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline within 24h | Load was acceptable | Repeat; consider lowering the box after a clean cycle |
| Still elevated next morning | Too much | Raise the box, hold, no progression this cycle |

### 3. Ceiling test — the falsifiable check on the upstream work

**Does the depth ceiling rise over cycles?**

- Box drops a notch per clean cycle → the glute-med work is winning.
- Ceiling won't move for **2–3 consecutive cycles** → capping depth is only managing the symptom. That's the signal to escalate to in-person assessment rather than keep grinding the same gate.

### 4. SL Glute Bridge → psoas lock — `S`

Free upgrade, zero added time. Pull the **free** knee above 90° and press both hands into it isometrically through each rep.

- **Primary:** glute max + hamstring (bridging side); psoas / iliacus + rectus femoris (locked side)
- **Compensations:** working-side hip dropping as you press — pelvis stays level; pelvis-drop terminator still ends the set
- **Regression:** hands light, free knee at 90° · **Progression:** hands off entirely

### 5. Frog Rock → `R` (Hips Tight)

`8 slow reps · 3s pause at end range`. Loaded adductor length at end range with **zero knee compression** — which is why it's on Rest day and why it's the right entry point regardless of whether the limiter is groin or knee.

- **Primary:** adductor magnus + brevis, hip capsule, hip internal rotators at length
- **Compensations:** lumbar flexion at the back end (stop the instant the low back rounds); knees wider than the hips tolerate (groin feels stretch, knee feels nothing)
- **Regression:** narrower knees, shorter rock · **Progression:** wider knees → 5s pause → light band around the thighs

First rung of the Cossack ladder.

## Considered and rejected

Reviewed against Movesmethod YouTube material, 2026-08-13. Documented so it isn't re-litigated.

| Item | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Nordic curls** | **No.** Kneeling load through the patellae + very large knee-flexor torque ≈ worst-case for a PFPS history. Kickstand RDL already covers eccentric posterior chain, hip-dominantly. |
| **Anterior-pelvic-tilt correction** | **No.** Weak evidence base; APT varies widely in asymptomatic people, and the wall test measures a motor task, not a structural finding. |
| **Standalone daily hip-mobility routine** | **No.** `W` already runs 18 min before every `S` and `C`. A parallel routine dilutes a working program. |
| **Cossack squat** | **Excluded indefinitely.** Demands exactly the deep loaded knee flexion this version caps. Entry rung (Frog Rock) went to `R`. |

### Reviewed — already covered, no change needed

- **Active hip rotation** → Shin-Box Get-Ups already does this (v13.3 replaced static 90/90).
- **Active end-range hip flexion** → the `W` Hip Flexor slot is Tuck L-Sit / Hanging Knee Raise, already loaded, not a passive stretch.

### What the source material got right

- *"A tight hamstring is not a short hamstring, it is a protected hamstring"* — correct and mainstream. Perceived tightness is largely stretch tolerance.
- **Neurodynamic differentiation** (slump / chin-tuck) — genuinely diagnostic. Ruled out here.
- **Strength through full ROM over passive stretching** — already the program's position via Mitchell and ATG since v11.

---

## Findings

- **Ankle dorsiflexion: 5 in (~12.7 cm) knee-to-wall.** Above the ~10 cm threshold — **ruled out as a limiter anywhere** (squat depth, Mǎbù, Step Down, Cossack). Any depth limit is hip or knee, never ankle. Makes **Kneeling Ankle the lowest-yield `W` item** — first candidate slot if one is ever needed.
- **Neural / sciatic contribution: ruled out** (no chin-tuck response).
- **Deep knee flexion, right: positive** — this version's subject.

## Open

- **Supine straddle angle** (adductor length) — knee-safe, non-blocking.
- **Active vs. passive SLR gap, each side** — knee-safe, non-blocking. Tunes the hip work only; the asymmetry is the interesting number given the right-side thread.
- Whether next-morning anterior knee soreness after `S` days has been present and filed as normal DOMS. If yes, existing depth was already too much and this squat only made it visible.

---

## Unchanged

Rotation `S → C → S → C → S → C → R`. All 14 `S` items mandatory, 2 working sets, v13.4 RIR + quality terminators intact. `W` still 12 items. `C` 60 min × 3/cycle. `N` collagen block. `D` daily habits. Foundations unchanged (Baar, Bellemans, Patrick/ATG, Mitchell, San Millán, Attia).

`C` is explicitly **unaffected and encouraged** — flat, high cadence, low resistance. Bellemans' cyclic cartilage loading. No grinding climbs or big-gear work while the knee settles.
